      The bottom line

      About this site

      The UK Debt Clock is an independent, non-partisan explainer. It takes no money, runs no ads and sets no cookies. Every figure comes from official statistics, and the code and data are open on GitHub. The data updates each month after the ONS public finances release.

      Spotted an error, or want to get in touch? Raise it on GitHub. Corrections are welcome.

      Sources and method

        How the figures are calculated

        • Debt everywhere on the page is general government gross debt (Maastricht definition): ONS series BKPX for the latest month, and the OBR public finances databank for yearly outturn and forecasts. The live share of GDP is measured against the GDP implied by the ONS net debt ratio.
        • The clock starts from the latest ONS month-end figure. It then adds debt at the recent-trend rate for the current financial year: the OBR's forecast rise in debt, plus this year's overspend against the OBR forecast so far, scaled up to a full year. It is an estimate between monthly releases, not an official figure.
        • The clock shows gross debt: general government consolidated gross debt on the Maastricht definition (ONS series BKPX). It adds the OBR's forecast rise in gross debt for the current year plus this year's overspend. Its share of GDP is measured against the same GDP as the ONS net debt figure. The per-person, per-household and household calculator figures at the top also use gross debt.
        • Live debt-to-GDP divides the clock by an estimate of GDP now. That GDP estimate starts from the GDP implied by the latest ONS debt ratio and grows at the OBR's forecast cash GDP growth for the current year. The changes are shown in percentage points of GDP.
        • Recent trend starts from the OBR forecast. For the current year it adds the overspend in borrowing so far, scaled up to a full year. For later years it adds the cost of three non-overlapping benefit groups (pensioner, health and disability, and other working-age benefits) growing at their 2019-20 to 2025-26 rate above inflation, instead of the OBR's rate. Interest on the extra debt is charged at the effective rate in the OBR forecast. It illustrates one risk. It is not a forecast.
        • Per person uses the ONS mid-2025 UK population. Per household uses the ONS 2025 households estimate.
        • Interest is central government debt interest, net of the Bank of England's Asset Purchase Facility. This is the OBR's measure. It counts what the government actually pays out, including inflation uplift on index-linked gilts.
        • Welfare is total UK welfare spending as defined in the OBR's Economic and Fiscal Outlook. That includes DWP benefits, Child Benefit, tax credits and Northern Ireland social security. Pensioner, working-age and children splits follow DWP's benefit summary table (Great Britain, DWP benefits).
        • Today's prices means 2026-27 prices, using the OBR's GDP deflator.
        • Visitor statistics: this site counts page views anonymously with Cloudflare Web Analytics. It sets no cookies and does not track individual visitors.
        • Opportunity cost compares the 2025-26 interest bill with the top-level spending functions in HM Treasury's PESA 2026 (Table 5.2, 2025-26 outturn, UK-wide, all levels of government). Only top-level functions are used, so no area is counted twice. "Welfare and social care" is the Social protection function. "Running government" is General public services minus public debt transactions, which is where debt interest sits.
